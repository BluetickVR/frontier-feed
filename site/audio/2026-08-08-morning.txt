Another paper, "Weights or Skills?", surveys robot-learning techniques, contrasting models with baked-in competence versus agents that write their own skills. This is a fundamental discussion for our long-term AI strategy. Are we building highly specialized models, or are we aiming for more adaptable agents that can learn and refine new capabilities on the fly? Understanding this dichotomy helps us shape our R&D roadmap.

Also in papers, "FactorJEPA" explores factorizing monolithic futures into layout-agent-interaction channels for crowded urban environments. As we explore more generative AI for property descriptions, image synthesis, or even virtual staging, the efficiency and quality of our tokenization will directly impact the performance and creativity of our generative models.

And finally on papers, "Activity Frames" discusses deterministic screen-activity compilation for agent memory and replay. Essentially, it's about agents remembering what a user *did*, not just what they *said*. For PropViz, this is huge. Imagine our agents learning from user interactions on the platform, not just their explicit queries, leading to much more personalized and proactive assistance for property searchers and sellers.
